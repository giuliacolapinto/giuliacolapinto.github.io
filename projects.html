Data Compression & Optimization

Tools: Python (Pandas, OpenPyXL)

Progressive compression algorithm applied to Walmart sales data, evolving through three levels — from basic Run-Length Encoding to Base 52 Alpha-Encoding to Global Pattern Tokenization — reducing file size by up to 36%.

Read more ▾

The project explores three custom algorithms to compress sales data from a Walmart dataset, reducing storage footprint of large Excel files by encoding numerical sequences into progressively more compact formats.

Compression Methods
  • Version 1 — Run-Length Encoding (RLE): Foundational approach. Instead of storing repeated numbers individually, the algorithm stores the value and its count using a mapping (zabcdefghi) to represent frequency. Spaces separate adjacent values.
  • Version 2 — Base 52 Alpha-Encoding: Evolves RLE by removing space delimiters (hidden byte-wasters). Frequencies are mapped to a Base 52 system (a-z, A-Z), packing more information per character (e.g., 10c instead of 10 10 10).
  • Version 3 — Global Pattern Tokenization: Instead of single-value repetitions, the algorithm scans the entire dataset for recurring triplet patterns. The 52 most frequent "sales patterns" are each assigned a unique single-character token, maximizing density through a structural data-mining approach.

AI vs Human: Writing Style Recognition

Tools: R (tidyverse, ggplot2, caret, tm, quanteda, tokenizers, MASS, class, tree) | R Markdown

NLP and Data Mining project to identify and distinguish AI-like vs Human-like writing styles. Rather than a simple AI detector, the goal is to track whether academic writing has drifted toward AI patterns following the widespread release of LLMs — validated on a historical arXiv abstracts dataset.

Read more ▾

The models are designed to recognize AI-like style (specific structures, vocabulary, sentence lengths) compared to a more natural human style. By mapping these differences, the project tracks how writing in human domains — specifically scientific research — has evolved post-LLM release.

Data Collection & Preprocessing
  • Base Dataset: Custom mixture of human-written texts and outputs from three LLM families: GPT-4o, LLaMA-3 (8B), and Gemma-2 (9B).
  • Text Normalization: via tm and quanteda — stemming, whitespace trimming, lowercasing, punctuation and number removal.
Feature Engineering
  • Mean sentence length and word count
  • Stopword usage ratio
  • Unique word count
  • TF-IDF representation of n-grams
  • Dimensionality reduction via PCA, retaining components explaining at least 70% of variance
Classification Models (Human = 0 | AI-Mix = 1)
  • LDA — Linear Discriminant Analysis
  • QDA — Quadratic Discriminant Analysis
  • Logistic Regression
  • KNN — K-Nearest Neighbors with automatic cross-validation for optimal K selection
  • Decision Trees
Validation & Real-World Application
  • External Essay Dataset: Student essays used to evaluate generalization of the style classifiers.
  • arXiv Scientific Papers: Historical abstracts split into two periods — 2007–2015 (pre-LLM) vs post-2022 — enabling temporal analysis of whether modern researchers are unconsciously adopting AI-like writing over time.

04

Rice Variety Classification

Tools: R | R Markdown

Data Mining project to classify two certified Turkish rice varieties (Cammeo vs Osmancik) from 7 morphological features extracted from 3,810 grain images. Logistic Regression outperformed K-NN in robustness and class separation.

Read more ▾

The dataset includes 3,810 grain images from the UCI Machine Learning Repository (Cinar & Koklu, 2019). Each grain is described by 7 morphological features measured from high-resolution images.

Features
  • Area: Number of pixels within the grain boundary.
  • Perimeter: Distance around the grain boundary.
  • Major Axis Length: Longest line drawable through the grain.
  • Minor Axis Length: Shortest line perpendicular to the major axis.
  • Eccentricity: Measure of how circular or elliptical the grain is.
  • Convex Area: Area of the smallest convex shell fitting the grain.
  • Extent: Ratio of grain area to its bounding box.
Methodology
  • Preprocessing: Binary encoding of target (1 = Cammeo, 0 = Osmancik).
  • Feature Scaling: Min-Max normalization for distance-based algorithms.
  • Model Comparison: K-NN vs Logistic Regression with hyperparameter tuning via validation set.
  • Evaluation: Accuracy, F1-Score, Log-Loss, ROC Curve analysis.
Key Results

Logistic Regression outperformed K-NN, showing higher robustness in separating the two classes. Detailed metrics and threshold analysis are documented in the compiled .html R Markdown report.

Cranial Clusters

Tools: R (tidyverse, Rmixmod, mclust, ggplot2, etc.)

Supervised clustering and classification project on the Howell cranial dataset to distinguish male and female samples. Gaussian Mixture Models with V-Fold Cross-Validation achieved 87% training accuracy, validated via Adjusted Rand Index (ARI).

Read more ▾
Pre-processing & Cleaning
  • Synostosis: Exclusion of 11 qualitative variables (e.g. RFA, RPA, BSA) due to null values from bone fusion in elderly subjects.
  • Standardization: Centering and scaling grouped by population/tribe to remove geographic bias.
  • Feature Selection: PCA to identify highest-loading variables (e.g. NAR, GOL, ZYB).
A. Gaussian Mixture Models — V-Fold Cross Validation
  • A.1 — 18 Original Variables: Iterative tests (100 simulations per V block) via Rmixmod on variables NAR, GOL, ZYB, BNL etc. CV blocks V = 5, 8, 10, 12, 15 evaluated. Best model by error: Gaussian_pk_L_C (constant volume, equal shape, free orientation) — CV error ≈ 0.122. Best model for accuracy: Gaussian_pk_Lk_C (variable volume) — peak accuracy 82.6%. Results stable across V values.
  • A.2 — PCA-Reduced (PC1 & PC2): Configurations V = 10, 20, 60, 100. Best models: EVE (Gaussian_pk_L_D_Ak_D) and EEV — CV error ≈ 0.145–0.146. Max accuracy ~81–82%. Misclassifications visualised via scatter plot with confidence ellipses.
B. K-Means Clustering (Unsupervised)

Tested as unsupervised baseline with K = 2.

  • Error Rate: 21.3%.
  • Adjusted Rand Index: 0.32.
C. Optimized Mclust Models
  • Search: Iterative search (200 simulations) for optimal subset of 10 variables and best probabilistic model.
  • Final Feature Set: MAB, FRC, AUB, GOL, NOL, AVR, JUB, ZYB, NPH, FMB.
  • Best Model: VVE (Variable Volume, Equal Shape, Equal Orientation).
  • Test Accuracy: 83%.

06

Labor Market Analytics

Tools: Tableau | R

Trend analysis of ~3 million Italian job advertisements (2019 & 2023) classified under the ESCO taxonomy. Developed a pipeline to track occupational demand shifts and skill evolution (digital, professional, transversal), identifying the key drivers of Italy's changing labor market.

Read more ▾

The analysis is grounded in Labour Market Intelligence (LMI), leveraging Big Data from online job advertisements (OJAs) published in Italy. Only postings from ESCO groups 1–4 were included (Managers, Professionals, Technicians, Clerical Workers), covering ~1.49M postings for 2019 and ~1.57M for 2023, across 162 occupations out of 421.

Research Objectives
  • Understand occupational demand dynamics and changes between 2019 and 2023.
  • Estimate the impact of skill requirements — digital, professional, and transversal/soft skills — identifying the most important and their relevance metrics.
  • Estimate the degree of horizontal and vertical skill overlap across occupations.
  • Measure the degree of novelty in Italian labor demand by comparing skill relevance across years.
  • Compare occupations based on required skill profiles.
Data & Methodology
  • Part 1 — Job Trends: 162 occupations with ≥ 1,000 total postings. Analysis of which occupations saw the largest absolute and percentage growth/decline.
  • Part 2 — Skill Analysis: 499 distinct skills (2019) → 538 (2023). For each of the 4 ESCO groups, the 12 highest-volume occupations were selected; 100 random postings per occupation per year (9,600 postings total). Skill types tracked: areaprof_aggregato, skill_type.
  • Part 3 — Skill Relevance: Relevance metrics computed for specific occupations — e.g., Statistical, Finance & Insurance Clerks (ESCO 4312) and Finance Managers (ESCO 1211) — to identify the most strategically important skills and their year-on-year shifts.
Stakeholders & Applications

Results relevant for: workers, HR departments, public employment services, headhunters, educational institutions, vocational training organizations, and policy makers.
